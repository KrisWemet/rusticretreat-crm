# Planning Pod runs venues from one record

Planning Pod is an all-in-one, browser-based venue platform that carries a wedding from website inquiry to day-of paperwork inside one linked record, and its deepest tools are the money and operations ones. An embeddable inquiry form with a built-in tour picker creates a Lead that moves through a configurable pipeline, can hold a date until a "Hold Until Date" that triggers an 8 am expiry email, and converts with one "Book Event" click into an Event whose headcount triad (estimated, actual, minimum) reprices proposal and invoice lines, the banquet event order (BEO) and the budget. Proposals carry categories, per-category service charges and per-guest pricing, lock on the first signature and change only through re-signed change orders; invoices copy the proposal in one click and collect through dated reminder emails or card auto-pay, while deposits, including card authorisation holds, stay out of revenue. Around the event sit a panel-and-layout BEO generator, a run-of-show that prints separately for each vendor, staff member or room, a floor-plan designer with tents, heaters, electrical elements and seating, an RSVP guest list, to-do templates anchored to event, inquiry or booking dates, and a client portal where couples sign, pay, answer questionnaires, manage guests and seat them until an edit lock a set number of days before the wedding. The weak edges are where couples and email meet the product: contracts are pasted text with signatures at the end (no documented initials, fill-in fields or signing order), custom form answers land in free-text notes, the lead activity log covers only 14 days, bulk email and two-way SMS are absent, free Gmail cannot connect, open tracking works only from an @planningpod.com address, the trigger-action workflow builder exists only in marketing copy, and vendor management, inventory and staff scheduling are thin or unproven. Most single-location venues pay $199 to $319 a month under pricing tied to how many events they manage at once, plus card fees ([Planning Pod homepage](https://planningpod.com/)), and reviewers — Capterra 4.3 from 38 reviews, G2 4.0 from 18 ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.ca/reviews/125947/planning-pod); [G2](https://www.g2.com/sellers/planning-pod)) — value the all-in-one money tools while faulting a dated interface, a learning curve and a portal some couples find confusing. Every finding comes from search-engine extracts of Planning Pod's own help center and marketing pages, because the research sandbox could not open planningpod.com directly.

## Search extracts, not screens, underpin every finding

The research sandbox's network policy blocked planningpod.com, help.planningpod.com and the app2.planningpod.com form host, so no Planning Pod page was read in full, no account was created and no screen was seen; every finding below comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, and menu paths and labels in quotation marks are as those extracts rendered them. Three tiers of evidence recur and are kept apart throughout. **Help-center articles** describe actual behaviour and are the strongest evidence, but none carries a date, and a few older copies survive on the former support.planningpod.com domain. **Marketing pages** show intent rather than proof, and several capabilities — the workflow engine, email sequences, inventory allocation, staff shift scheduling — exist only there. **Third-party reviews** rest on about 56 reviews across Capterra and G2, and many comparison pieces are written by competitors such as Perfect Venue. The help center visibly lags the product in at least one area: it documents connecting your own Stripe, Square or PaySimple account, while the current features page says "Planning Pod includes integrated payments through Adyen" ([Planning Pod features](https://planningpod.com/features)). "Not found" in this report therefore means absent from the retrieved extracts, not proven missing. The absences that would change a build decision — whether conflicts block a save, whether bookings carry setup buffers, and what the workflow builder can actually do — are worth confirming in a Planning Pod demo.

## Inquiries flow from an embedded form to a dated hold and "Book Event"

### The inquiry form is a block builder with seven automatic on-submit actions

Inquiry forms are built at Settings > Lead Management > Lead Capture Forms, and a venue can keep **several forms for different inquiry types**, each with its own default lead source and starting pipeline step ([help: multiple lead capture forms](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating-multiple-lead-capture-forms-for-different-purposes); [help: lead capture form settings](https://planningpod.com/help-center/lead-capture-form-settings)). Any form can also replace the staff "Add Lead" screen through a "Replace Default 'Add Lead' Form" toggle, so phone and walk-in inquiries use the same fields as web ones ([help: custom internal Add Lead form](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating-a-custom-internal-add-lead-form)). The builder stacks blocks: static headlines, text and dividers, and data blocks for **additional contacts (how a second partner or a parent is captured), preferred and alternate event dates, estimated headcount, event budget, venue-space requests, tour scheduling and custom questions** ([help: content blocks](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-content-blocks-to-lead-capture-forms); [help: estimated headcount](https://planningpod.com/help-center/lead-capture-setup-adding-estimated-headcount)). Custom questions take short answer, long answer, radio, dropdown or checkbox formats, each optional or required, but **their answers are written into the lead's Notes rather than into fields**, so they cannot be filtered or reported on ([help: custom questions](https://planningpod.com/help-center/lead-capture-setup-adding-custom-questions)). Design settings and custom fonts control appearance ([help: embeddable form design](https://planningpod.com/help-center/lead-capture-setup-embeddable-form-design); [help: custom fonts](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-custom-fonts-to-the-lead-capture-form)).

Each form publishes as a hosted share link or as a "Website Embed Code" snippet that the help center calls an advanced step for a web developer ([help: setting up lead capture](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-up-lead-capture/collection-for-your-website)). Live forms load from app2.planningpod.com with a parameter that decodes to a "share" or "embed" version plus the form's UUID ([live form example](https://app2.planningpod.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=leads.interestForm&ssid=eyJWRVJTSU9OIjoic2hhcmUiLCJVVUlEIjoiNEVFNTQ2MjAtQkNFQi00MEY5LTNGOTFEMjkwNzNDNEM4OTYifQ%3D%3D)), and deleting a form removes it from every page that embeds it, so the embed renders live rather than as a copy ([help: sharing lead forms](https://planningpod.com/help-center/sharing-lead-forms-with-clients)). Every submission fires the form's **"default actions": set the lead source, set the pipeline step, apply tags, assign staff ("Assign Users to New Lead"), email chosen staff ("When Lead is Captured"), send the "Automated Reply to New Lead" template, and import a to-do template** ([help: what happens when a lead is captured](https://planningpod.com/help-center/lead-capture-setup-what-happens-when-a-lead-is-captured)). The auto-reply is one of several system emails — with the login invitation, the new-user welcome and the "lead hold about to expire" warning — that can be edited but not personalised per send ([help: customizing default emails](https://planningpod.com/help-center/customizing-default-email-messages)). New leads land in the Leads list, where default sorting can push them to page 2; the documented fix is an Advanced Search sorted by "Captured Date (Descending)" and saved as the default "Quick View" ([help: lead not showing](https://planningpod.com/help-center/why-is-my-lead-not-showing-in-my-lead-pipeline)).

Marketplace leads arrive by parsing email. Turning on "Capture Your Wedding Pro Leads (The Knot + Wedding Wire)" and connecting the mailbox registered with those sites lets Planning Pod turn each "New Lead" notification into a Lead. The help center itself calls this "not a true integration", warns that the client's address can be a marketplace relay, and says WeddingWire format changes can stop imports until support receives the latest notification ([help: The Knot + WeddingWire](https://planningpod.com/help-center/capturing-leads-from-the-knot-wedding-wire)), even though the sales comparison page markets The Knot and WeddingWire as built-in integrations ([Planning Pod vs Perfect Venue](https://planningpod.com/planning-pod-vs-perfect-venue)). Zapier templates create leads from Squarespace, Google Forms, Wix, Gravity Forms, Ninja Forms, Formidable, HubSpot and Tally ([Zapier: Squarespace to Planning Pod](https://zapier.com/apps/planning-pod/integrations/squarespace-forms/1598719/create-new-planning-pod-leads-from-new-squarespace-forms-form-submissions)). Marketing pairs the form with an embedded availability calendar ([event booking calendar](https://planningpod.com/event-booking-calendar)), but no source says the date blocks check real availability, and **no spam protection, duplicate detection, thank-you message or redirect setting is documented**.

### Contacts are the only CRM entity, with no custom fields or deduplication

Planning Pod's CRM is one Contacts address book shared by clients, leads, staff, vendors, subcontractors and volunteers ([help: how contacts work](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-contacts-work)). A contact holds name, company (free text, not a linked organisation), title and email, plus multiple emails, phones, websites and addresses, notes, tags, files and an access role ([help: adding a contact](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-a-new-contact)). Tags are the only grouping device — the help center's example tags contacts "Vendors" and "Transportation", searches for both and exports the result — and the list pages at 100 contacts ([help: how contacts work](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-contacts-work)). Imports accept CSV, with column mapping and tags applied to the whole batch, or vCard, which the help center calls "much more stable" ([help: importing contacts](https://planningpod.com/help-center/importing-contacts-from-a-file)).

The Lead is the pre-booking record. Contacts are "assigned" to it under "Contacts, Vendors & Venues" — couple, parents and coordinator alike, with no formal owner field — and it stores the preferred event date ([help: assigning contacts to leads](https://planningpod.com/help-center/assigning-contacts-to-leads)). Each lead carries the tools an event will use (Appointments, Files, Forms Builder, Notes, Proposals, Contracts, To-Do's, Worksheets and, on Venue Management plans, Food & Beverage) plus colour-coded counts of key, upcoming, overdue and waiting items ([help, older copy: lead dashboards](https://support.planningpod.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015943788-Using-Lead-Dashboards)). History is shallow: the **Activity Log covers only the last 14 days** of user actions and emails sent from the lead, with older history in a User Activity Report under System Reporting ([help: lead dashboards](https://planningpod.com/help-center/using-lead-dashboards); [help: activity logs](https://planningpod.com/help-center/activity-logs-viewing-user-activity-and-email-history)). **Configurable custom fields, organisation records, duplicate detection or merging, and lead scoring were all not found.** "Duplicating or Copying a Lead" is a copy function ([help: events and leads index](https://planningpod.com/help-center/events-and-leads)), call logging appears only in marketing copy ([communications & collaboration](https://planningpod.com/solutions/use-cases/communications-and-collaboration)), and a reviewer says search "could use some more work" ([Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/125947/Planning-Pod/reviews/)).

### A linear pipeline with idle alerts sits beside a separate hold-and-close status

Each lead occupies one step of a configurable, linear "Sales Pipeline". Admins add, rename, reorder and disable steps and give each an **"idle" threshold in days** after which the system flags a stuck lead; the default pipeline ends in an "Idle" step, and **a step that has ever held a lead cannot be deleted** because past reporting depends on it ([help: custom sales pipeline](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating-a-custom-lead/sales-pipeline)). Two help articles give different menu paths for this screen, which suggests it has moved ([help: how leads work](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-leads-work)). Whether the pipeline renders as a kanban board was not confirmed.

A separate **Status panel** places the lead on hold, edits its source or closes it ([help: how leads work](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-leads-work)). "Place On Hold" covers the lead's date, time and venue and takes a **"Hold Until Date"**; chosen contacts receive an automated email **at 8 am on that date**, the expiry shows in Calendars and on the Home Page Dashboard, and expired holds surface as overdue items ([help: placing a hold](https://planningpod.com/help-center/placing-a-hold-for-a-lead); [help: home page dashboard](https://planningpod.com/help-center/home-page-dashboard)). Marketing adds soft and hard holds and conflict alerts against held dates ([platform](https://planningpod.com/platform); [event booking calendar](https://planningpod.com/event-booking-calendar)), but whether a hold blocks other leads from the date or merely warns is undocumented. Closing a lead records a reason: the **Leads Lost Summary** exports date closed, reason and the step where the lead was lost, and **Leads Won** records the conversion date ([help: leads reporting](https://planningpod.com/help-center/leads-reporting)); whether lost reasons come from a picklist is unknown. Conversion is a **"Book Event"** button whose panel adjusts dates and times, sets the event type and chooses how attendees are managed, then creates an Event carrying all the lead's data ([help, older copy: lead dashboards](https://support.planningpod.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015943788-Using-Lead-Dashboards)). Follow-up nudges come from idle alerts, capture-time to-do templates and the dashboard counts, and marketing also claims automations that send intro emails, move stages and assign staff ([CRM use case](https://planningpod.com/solutions/use-cases/crm)).

### Tours book themselves inside the inquiry form

Site tours are a "Schedule a Tour" block inside an inquiry form ("Schedule an Appointment" on event-type accounts), so a tour booking creates or attaches to a Lead and tour-first couples enter the pipeline automatically. The block is a small slot engine with **blocked weekdays and dates, a cap on simultaneous tours, a booking horizon, available hours, tour duration, a buffer between tours, and up to three email or text reminders** ([help: scheduling a tour](https://planningpod.com/help-center/lead-capture-setup-scheduling-a-tour)). Tours appear on the master calendar and in the lead's Appointments tool, and staff-added appointments notify every contact assigned to the lead or event ([help: calendars tool](https://planningpod.com/help-center/getting-around-the-calendars-tool); [help: adding an appointment](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-an-appointment-to-the-calendar)). Live venue forms word the outcome differently — one says a submitted tour is scheduled automatically, another that it is "only a request" awaiting staff confirmation — so confirmation behaviour appears to be set by each venue ([live form example](https://app2.planningpod.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=leads.interestForm&ssid=eyJWRVJTSU9OIjoiZW1iZWQiLCJVVUlEIjoiNEI1QzYwQkEtRTYzNy01NDNBLTEzRUFFMDREMjRGREVCQTEifQ%3D%3D)). Shareable booking links are still described as **"coming soon"** ([event booking calendar](https://planningpod.com/event-booking-calendar)), with Calendly and Acuity available through Zapier ([Zapier: Calendly](https://zapier.com/apps/planning-pod/integrations/calendly)). Self-serve reschedule or cancel links, round-robin staff assignment and tour sync to staff calendars were not found.

## Email is the weakest link, and automation is mostly hard-wired

### A shared inbox auto-links mail but forces a choice between branding and tracking

The Communications dashboard is a shared inbox. Venues either connect mailboxes at Settings > Communications & Alerts > Communications Setup (Email) > Connect Email, choosing which users may send from each address and setting signatures and privacy, or skip connection, send through the system address and route client replies to a chosen contact's own inbox with a reply-to setting ([help: setting up your email address](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-up-your-email-address-in-planning-pod); [help: email setup](https://planningpod.com/help-center/how-to-set-up-your-email-communications)). **Google connects only for Google Workspace accounts — free Gmail cannot — and a Workspace admin must approve Planning Pod as an OAuth app** ([help: connecting Google email](https://planningpod.com/help-center/connecting-your-google-email-account-to-planning-pod)). The inbox pulls in any message from an address belonging to a Contact, supports refresh, starring, archiving and "Clear from Inbox", and links messages to Events or Leads automatically, with manual link and unlink and a setting to switch auto-linking off ([help: communications dashboard](https://planningpod.com/help-center/using-the-communications-dashboard); [blog: enhanced communications](https://planningpod.com/blog/planning-pods-enhanced-communications-tool-empowers-event-pros)). The troubleshooting page names the failure modes: generic lead names such as "Birthday" attract other events' mail, and shared mailboxes connected by several users appear in duplicate ([help: troubleshooting email](https://planningpod.com/help-center/troubleshooting-common-email-challenges-in-your-planning-pod-account)).

The sharpest constraint is tracking. **Email History shows processed, delivered and opened status only for mail sent from the system @planningpod.com address; mail from a connected, branded mailbox is untracked** ([help: activity logs](https://planningpod.com/help-center/activity-logs-viewing-user-activity-and-email-history)), and invoice emails follow the same rule ([help: emailing invoices](https://planningpod.com/help-center/emailing-invoices-to-clients)). Messages written in the Communications tool also cannot carry a signature request or a "Pay Now" button, which work only from each document's own send flow ([help: emailing documents from Communications](https://planningpod.com/help-center/emailing-a-proposal-invoice-or-contract-from-the-communications-tool)). Reviewers are blunt — "They want you to integrate your email into the platform, don't do it, it is awful" — and report messages filed under the wrong account and sent emails reappearing as new inbox items ([Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/125947/Planning-Pod/reviews/); [G2 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/planning-pod/reviews)). G2 rates its email automation 6.0 against Perfect Venue's 9.3, though on only 8 reviews ([G2 comparison](https://www.g2.com/compare/perfect-venue-vs-planning-pod)).

### Templates merge event data, but there is no bulk send or two-way SMS

Email templates live at Templates > Communication Templates > Email Templates > "Add Template" (title, subject, body), and each system notification can be assigned a default template ([help: default email templates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-default-email-templates-for-notifications)). "Smart Text Merge with Data Placeholders" fills event data into email, proposal, invoice and contract templates ([help: smart text merge](https://planningpod.com/help-center/smart-text-merge-with-data-placeholders)); the placeholder list itself was not retrieved. **No bulk or segment email exists in the help center**, and a reviewer confirms each recipient must be added one by one even when contacts share a tag ([Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/125947/Planning-Pod/reviews/)); list marketing goes through Zapier to tools such as Constant Contact or Flodesk ([Zapier: Constant Contact](https://zapier.com/apps/planning-pod/integrations/constant-contact)). Text messages are outbound reminders only, for tours, tasks, itinerary items and payments ([features](https://planningpod.com/features); [help: scheduling a tour](https://planningpod.com/help-center/lead-capture-setup-scheduling-a-tour)). **No two-way SMS, internal comment threads or @mentions** were documented, which leaves each lead's Notes tool as the only internal-notes surface; scheduled send, conversation threading and the Outlook connection method were also not found.

### Documented automations are fixed-purpose; the workflow builder is only marketed

The automations the help center actually documents are single-purpose: lead-capture default actions, the 8 am hold-expiry email, per-step idle alerts, tour reminders, appointment notices, to-do templates that compute due dates and preset email or SMS reminders, and invoice payment reminders ([help: setting up lead capture](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-up-lead-capture/collection-for-your-website); [help: placing a hold](https://planningpod.com/help-center/placing-a-hold-for-a-lead); [help: to-do templates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating-using-to-do-list-templates); [help: payment reminders](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-up-payment-reminders-for-invoices)). Marketing describes a general engine of single or multi-step automations triggered by a new lead, a reserved room, a task due date, a signed proposal or contract, or a received payment, with actions such as sending a template, generating a document, creating an event, creating and sending an invoice, starting an email sequence, or auto-responding to incoming payments ([automations & insights](https://planningpod.com/solutions/use-cases/automations-and-insights)). Sequences are pitched as a series of templates, a send schedule and start and stop triggers — starting on a new lead and stopping when the lead replies, for example — "ideal for lead follow-ups and periodic outreach to past clients" ([communications & collaboration](https://planningpod.com/solutions/use-cases/communications-and-collaboration); [sales department](https://planningpod.com/solutions/departments/sales)), and marketing says the system reminds staff about unsigned documents ([contracts & e-signatures](https://planningpod.com/contracts-and-e-signatures)). **No help article documents the workflow or sequence builder**, so its trigger list, delay units, branching and stop-on-reply logic are unverified, and the help center still describes turning a proposal into an invoice as a manual click.

## Headcount-linked line items make the money tools the deepest layer

### Proposals price by guest count and lock on the first signature

A proposal starts with client or lead, address, auto-generated number, name and date; its dashboard then holds introduction and terms text, line items, categories, fees, attachments such as brochures, an image gallery, tags and a client message ([help: adding a proposal](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-a-new-proposal)). The terms block prints near the top of the PDF so that, in the help center's words, "you can use a Proposal as a signed contract" ([help: introductory/contractual text](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-introductory/contractual-text-to-a-proposal)). Each **line item** has quantity or hours (at least 1), unit cost, **one tax rate and one markup or discount percentage** ([help: proposal line item](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-a-proposal-line-item)). Its quantity can instead follow one of the event's **three headcounts — estimated, actual or minimum**, the minimum being "mainly used for billing by venues and caterers" — and the line reprices whenever that headcount changes on the Event Dashboard or in the BEO ([help: headcounts](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-using-headcounts-for-events)). Lines group into categories that subtotal cost, tax and markup automatically, and **a category can hide its line amounts so only the subtotal shows**, which is how package pricing is presented ([help: line item categories](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding/managing-line-item-categories-for-invoices); [help: category topics](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-line-item-category-topics-for-proposals/invoices)).

Fees and tax are unusually granular. **Additional Fees** — service charge, gratuity, delivery or shipping, setup or cleanup — are a percentage or a flat amount. Placed in the totals they apply to the whole document; placed in a category's subtotal area they apply only there ("the 18% will only be calculated against all line items in that category"); and each fee toggles whether its base includes line taxes and markups and can carry its own tax rate, because some jurisdictions tax gratuities ([help: additional fees](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-additional-fees-to-proposals)). Fees can be preset in Settings ([help: fees on invoices](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-additional-fees-to-invoices)); an item subject to two taxes needs a combined rate, and an account slider decides whether markups and discounts are taxed ([help: tax rates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/customizing-the-tax-rates-for-your-account)). Proposals save as templates, link Food & Beverage package templates on venue plans, and import reusable Line Item Templates for packages, menu items and rentals ([help: proposal templates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating/using-proposal-templates); [help: line item templates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating-using-line-item-templates)).

Signing starts from a "Signers" list. Staff email each signer a link to "a secure Web page (outside of your account)" where the signer reviews the document and clicks "I Agree", or use "Collect Signature in Person"; every signer then receives the signed PDF, which records **legal name, date and IP address** ([help: proposal e-signatures](https://planningpod.com/help-center/collecting-electronic-signatures-for-proposals)). Separate default emails cover the signature request and the PDF delivery ([help: customizing default emails](https://planningpod.com/help-center/customizing-default-email-messages)). In the portal, the couple sees "Action Required", clicks "Sign Now", and the venue is notified ([help: portal signatures](https://planningpod.com/help-center/collecting-signatures-using-the-client-portal)). **The first signature locks the proposal.** Edits then require a **Change Order** from the "Proposal Locked" area, which relocks once its first new signature arrives, while earlier signed versions stay downloadable ([help: change orders](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating-a-change-order-for-a-signed-proposal)). Help-center searches found **no client-selectable optional items, expiry dates, proposal-level discount or open and view tracking**.

### Contracts are pasted text with end-of-document signatures and an audit stamp

The separate Contracts tool is text-first. Templates sit under Templates → Business & Legal Templates → Contracts as a name plus pasted legal text, and **an existing PDF cannot be attached** — "you will not be able to attach a PDF or electronic file of any pre-existing legal language" ([help: how contracts work](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-contracts-work)). "Import Template as New Contract" accepts one or more templates, implying clause-by-clause assembly ([help: contract templates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating-using-contract-templates)), and placeholders merge event data plus **up to three signers' data points** ([help: smart text merge](https://planningpod.com/help-center/smart-text-merge-with-data-placeholders)). The dashboard lists "Intended Signers", and the help center tells venues to add **at least one internal counter-signer, who receives notifications of all signing activity** ([help: contract e-signatures](https://planningpod.com/help-center/collecting-electronic-signatures-for-contracts)). Signers sign by emailed link, in person or in the portal; every signer receives the signed PDF, and the dashboard's "Signed" notice shows **legal name, date, IP address and a "unique signature fingerprint"** ([help: how contracts work](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-contracts-work)). BEOs are a third signable document ([help: editing a BEO](https://planningpod.com/help-center/editing-a-beo-inside-an-event)). Planning Pod's terms state that it does not verify signers' identity and that electronic signatures may not be enforceable for every document ([terms and conditions](https://planningpod.com/terms-and-conditions)).

For a gap analysis the absences matter more than the features. **No retrieved source documents signing order, initials per page or per clause, fields or checkboxes the client fills in, the capture method (drawn, typed or click), a certificate-of-completion page, or locking and change orders for contracts** as opposed to proposals. A venue agreement with a dozen initials blocks and boxes that both parties complete goes beyond anything Planning Pod documents, and a CRM that enforces a venue-then-couple signing order is more structured than Planning Pod's notified counter-signer.

### Invoices copy the proposal and collect through dated reminders or auto-pay

Invoices reuse the proposal engine. A new invoice takes client, address, auto number, name, creation date, due date and an optional P.O. number ([help: adding an invoice](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-a-new-invoice)); **"Create Invoice" on a proposal copies every line item, category and Additional Fee**, asks whether to bring attachments and gallery photos, and opens the due date, tags and scheduled payments for editing ([help: invoice from proposal](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating-an-invoice-from-a-proposal)). Invoice templates are reusable, and invoices and receipts carry the venue's logo and brand colour ([help: invoice templates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating/using-invoice-templates); [invoicing & payments](https://planningpod.com/invoicing-and-payments)).

Instalments work two ways, and both **require a supported processor** ([help: how invoices work](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-invoices-work)). **Payment reminders** are dated entries on the invoice: staff set the first reminder's date and time, a "day span" and an amount; a batch shares one interval and one amount, so unequal instalments such as 25/25/50 must be entered one at a time; each email states amount and due date, attaches the invoice PDF and can include "Pay Now"; and each entry shows "pending" until it sends, with a paid mark cancelling it ([help: payment reminders](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-up-payment-reminders-for-invoices)). **Auto-pay** schedules charges against a card the client saved in advance ([help: auto-pay](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-up-auto-pay-credit-card-payments-for-invoices)), and switching processors deletes every scheduled automatic payment ([help: payment processing setup](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-up-online-electronic-payment-processing-in-planning-pod)). **No rule that computes instalment dates or amounts from the event date is documented**; schedules are concrete dated amounts. Offline money goes through "Manually Enter Payment" with date, amount and reference number ([help: adding a payment](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-an-invoice-payment)), **offline refunds are entered as negative payments** with "refund" in the reference ([help: refunds](https://planningpod.com/help-center/how-do-i-refund-or-partially-refund-a-payment-on-an-invoice)), and staff key phone or in-person cards through "Add Payment/Process Payment" ([help: card payments](https://planningpod.com/help-center/collecting-online-credit-card-payments-for-invoices)).

Overdue invoices show in red on the Home Page Dashboard with a look-back of 7 to 180 days or all time ([help: home page dashboard](https://planningpod.com/help-center/home-page-dashboard)), but **no invoice status field, late-fee automation, credit note or client statement** was found. Users call invoicing "easy to set and forget", yet report that **only one person can view an invoice** — one venue was told to have the bride share her login so her parents could pay — along with no clear way to delete an amount and no quarter-end report of taxes owed ([Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/125947/Planning-Pod/reviews/); [G2 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/planning-pod/reviews)).

### Deposits stay apart from revenue, including card authorisation holds

Deposits are first-class objects: "Track Deposit" records one, and "Deposit Actions" → "Apply Deposit Towards Amount Owed" applies all or part of it, with partial refunds tracked ([help: deposits](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-managing-deposits)). Through Stripe, a deposit request is either an **authorisation-only hold**, which moves no money and must be reauthorised every 6 days until released or applied, or **"Authorization + Capture"**, which takes the money but keeps it apart from invoice payments "so you can accurately track your deposit and keep it separate from your actual revenue". An applied Stripe deposit shows in the invoice's taxes and fees section rather than as a payment, and deposit requests can be scheduled like reminders ([help: payment reminders vs deposit requests](https://planningpod.com/help-center/stripe-payments-in-planning-pod-payment-reminders-vs.-deposit-requests)). One deposits extract says card deposits are not processed at all, which reads as text written before the Stripe feature ([help: deposits](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-managing-deposits)). For a venue that takes refundable damage deposits, the separation from revenue and the card hold are capabilities most basic CRMs lack.

### One processor is active at a time, and the processor story is in flux

The help center documents bring-your-own processing from Settings → "Payment Processing", with **only one processor active** at a time ([help: payment processing setup](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-up-online-electronic-payment-processing-in-planning-pod)). Current marketing instead describes integrated payments that need no separate processor, and a third-party API report says card data is handled by Adyen or QuickBooks Payments ([Supergood API report](https://supergood.ai/api-report-card/planning-pod)). The two generations of documentation disagree on rates and options:

Clients pay three ways: through a "Pay Now" button on an emailed invoice or reminder (staff tick "Add 'Pay Now' Button to This Email?" and the click opens a card form in a new tab); through the portal, where "Make Payment Now" pays a scheduled instalment and "Make Payment" pays any amount from methods saved under "My Payment Methods"; or through auto-pay ([help: card payments](https://planningpod.com/help-center/collecting-online-credit-card-payments-for-invoices); [help: for clients](https://planningpod.com/help-center/for-clients-what-is-the-client-portal-and-how-do-i-use-it); [help: payment methods](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-payment-methods-in-the-client-portal)). Staff take in-person payments and holds on a Stripe Terminal ([help: Stripe Terminal](https://planningpod.com/help-center/stripe-terminal-setting-up-your-terminal-and-collecting-payments)). **Card surcharging is optional, applies to cards only "where legally permitted", and is added on top** — a $1,000 invoice at 3% charges $1,030 ([help: surcharges with Stripe](https://planningpod.com/help-center/how-credit-card-surcharges-and-payment-processing-work-in-planning-pod-using-stripe)) — although an older article limits fee pass-through to PaySimple accounts. The same setup article says Planning Pod "is unable to track these fees" and that changing processors forces clients to re-enter stored cards ([help: payment processing setup](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-up-online-electronic-payment-processing-in-planning-pod)), and refunds through the platform are possible only within 60 days of the transaction, at least for ticket orders ([help: refunding a ticket order](https://planningpod.com/help-center/refunding-a-registration-ticket-order)). **Nothing documents Interac e-Transfer, Canadian dollars or multi-currency**, so a Canadian venue would log e-Transfers as manual payments.

### Packages price per head, fixed or by item, with upcharges but no seasonal rates

On Venue Management plans one library holds food, beverage and equipment items with images, descriptions, unit price, wholesale cost, portion size, dietary notes, recipes and ingredients, which combine into reusable package and equipment templates ([F&B tools](https://planningpod.com/food-and-beverage-tools)). **Packages price per head, at a fixed cost, or as the sum of item prices**; per-head customer-choice packages can carry **per-item upcharges** that add to the per-head price when chosen, and venues can **hide package items** on proposals and invoices ([blog: catering package technology](https://planningpod.com/blog/planning-pod-catering-package-technology-gives-users-new-levels-of-customization)). Items can be switched inactive ([help: F&B items](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding/managing-food-beverage-items)), and rentals otherwise live as line-item templates. The portal now notifies the venue when clients pick packages (same blog), but whether couples choose options themselves or staff record their choices is unconfirmed. **No seasonal, day-of-week or year-based rates, minimum-spend rules, or space-rental fees tied to the calendar were found**; Planning Pod's blog post on seasonal "dynamic pricing" is content marketing with no matching feature in the help center ([blog: dynamic pricing](https://planningpod.com/blog/dynamic-pricing-for-event-and-venue-businesses-using-data-to-optimize-revenue-across-seasons)).

### Budgets track estimated, negotiated and actual cost per line

Each event's Budget tool groups lines into categories with subtotals, with uncategorised lines in a table at the top ([help: how budgets work](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-budgets-work)). Every line carries **estimated, negotiated and actual amounts**, each a fixed figure, a percentage of the event's target budget, or driven by a "Budgeting Group" such as headcount or quantity; under Event Headcounts, the estimated count drives the estimated and negotiated amounts and the actual count drives the actual ([help: budget line items](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding/managing-budget-line-items); [help: headcounts](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-using-headcounts-for-events)). Lines track vendor payments with due dates, reminders, assigned vendors or staff and notes, and the tool totals budgeted cost, paid to date and balance due ([help: budget payment due dates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding/managing-payment-due-dates-and-reminders-for-budget-line-items)). Budgets hold revenue lines too, import from CSV (Item Name, Item Type, Estimated, Negotiated, Actual, Note) and export PDF and CSV reports including an event profit-and-loss statement ([event budget planner](https://planningpod.com/budgeting); [help: budget import](https://planningpod.com/help-center/uploading-or-importing-budget-data-from-a-file-spreadsheet)). A reviewer's wish for invoices linked to the budget indicates that **client invoices do not feed budget revenue**, so per-wedding profit depends on re-keying revenue ([Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/125947/Planning-Pod/reviews/)).

### Files live per event, and every document renders to PDF

Each event has a Files tool and contacts take attachments; files cannot be edited inside the app except linked Google Docs ([help: file storage](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-does-file-storage-/-sharing-work); [help: contact files](https://planningpod.com/help-center/attaching-files-to-a-contact)). Couples **upload and download through a Files tile in the portal** — "Files allows you to upload files to share with your event professional/venue and download files they have shared with you" — which appears only when the tool is enabled for portal users and the client is assigned to the event ([help: for clients](https://planningpod.com/help-center/for-clients-what-is-the-client-portal-and-how-do-i-use-it)). A central Business & Legal Templates library holds contract, proposal, invoice and line-item templates, all duplicable ([help: duplicating templates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/duplicating-templates)). PDFs come out of proposals, signed contracts, invoices (attached to every reminder), BEOs, itineraries, worksheets, to-do lists, floor plans and the event's contact, vendor and venue exports. **Storage limits, folders, version history and per-file permissions are undocumented.**

## One event record feeds the BEO, run-of-show, floor plan and guest list

### The calendar layers spaces, holds, blackouts and tours, with one-way sync

Adding a venue requires an initial space defined by **name, colour, maximum capacity and an associated floor-plan template**, and each venue, space, lead and staff member gets an automatic sub-calendar that feeds one master Calendars tool ([help: venue locations](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding/managing-venue-locations-in-your-account); [help: how calendars work](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-calendars-work)); marketing claims unlimited venues and spaces ([event booking calendar](https://planningpod.com/event-booking-calendar)). Views are **Venue Timeline (spaces as rows), List, Month, Week and Day**, and filters cut by lifecycle ("Open Leads, Holds, Booked Events, Archived Events/Closed Leads") and by item type (event dates, venue bookings, blackouts, tours, appointments, F&B dates and to-dos) ([help: calendars tool](https://planningpod.com/help-center/getting-around-the-calendars-tool)). Staff add tours, appointments, to-dos and **venue blackouts** straight from the calendar and drag items to new days, except repeating events; colour comes from the space and from each event ([help: adding an event](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-a-new-event)). Spaces are booked on the Event Dashboard by assigning one or more venues with the dates and times each is used; bookings appear on both the event's own calendar and the master calendar and export as an "Assigned Venues" PDF ([help: event dashboards](https://planningpod.com/help-center/using-event-dashboards)). The lifecycle runs open lead, hold, booked event, archived; no "Cancelled" status appears, and whether a space's capacity is checked against the headcount is unknown.

Conflict detection is a **red or green indicator on the event with a "View conflict" link** ([help: event dashboards](https://planningpod.com/help-center/using-event-dashboards)). Marketing says alerts fire when a day, time and space clash with a booked event, lead or hold and that alert rules are configurable ([event booking calendar](https://planningpod.com/event-booking-calendar)), but the configuration article was not retrieved, so whether a conflict blocks saving is unknown. **Setup and teardown buffers on bookings are undocumented** — only tour buffers exist — even though Planning Pod's own blog calls them a must-have ([blog: foolproof booking guide](https://planningpod.com/blog/foolproof-guide-to-managing-event-bookings-and-avoiding-double-bookings)), and partial-day blackouts per space appear only in marketing ([event & wedding venues](https://planningpod.com/solutions/industries/event-and-wedding-venues)). Sync is **one-way**: "Subscribe to Planning Pod Calendars" publishes a view-only feed to Google, Outlook, Apple or Yahoo that "WILL NOT pull your other e-calendar items into Planning Pod" and refreshes whenever the external calendar polls, while .ics import and export are one-off ([help: sync to Google and Outlook](https://planningpod.com/help-center/how-to-sync-your-planning-pod-calendar-to-google-and-to-outlook); [help: calendar import/export](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-calendar-import-export-options)). A website **availability embed** shows bookings by event or by venue, includes or excludes leads, holds, booked and archived items, respects business hours, and shows blackouts only in by-venue mode ([help: calendars on your website](https://planningpod.com/help-center/displaying-planning-pod-calendars-on-your-website)). Repeat events are limited to the Venue 50+ and Enterprise 50+ packages ([help: repeat events](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-repeat-events-in-planning-pod)).

### Event records carry the headcount triad, copy with shifted dates and add worksheets

An event is created with name, colour, event type, optional target budget and start and end times ([help: adding an event](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-a-new-event)); its dashboard shows status, booking date, type and budget, and "Edit" changes name, description, colour, dates and thumbnail ([help: event dashboards](https://planningpod.com/help-center/using-event-dashboards)). The **headcount triad** is the operational spine: estimated, actual (final) and minimum counts feed proposal and invoice lines, the BEO and the budget, and **editing a headcount inside the BEO pushes the change back to the dashboard and every connected document**; lead headcounts, by contrast, are typed by hand and do not pull from the guest list ([help: headcounts](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-using-headcounts-for-events)). Contacts join an event with account access, portal access or tracking only ([help: event access](https://planningpod.com/help-center/assigning-contacts-to-events-/-managing-event-access)); a Vendors tab lists vendors; overdue to-dos appear in red with edit, complete and reminder-email actions; and an Activity Log records what users did inside the event ([help: event dashboards](https://planningpod.com/help-center/using-event-dashboards)). Archived events cannot be reopened for **two weeks**, and auto-archiving is a setting ([help: archiving](https://planningpod.com/help-center/archiving-an-event)).

"Duplicate" copies an event to a new start date, letting staff choose which tools come along and **shifting every dated item relative to the new date**, while clearing RSVP statuses, registrations, budget payments and invoice payments ([help: duplicating an event](https://planningpod.com/help-center/duplicating-or-copying-an-event)). Most tools also offer "Save as Template" and template import, and a central Templates menu covers budgets, checklists, floor plans, itineraries, to-do lists and vision boards ([help: duplicating templates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/duplicating-templates)). **Worksheets** are named free-text pages for "all the little details" of one part of the event — a transportation sheet with vehicle details is the help center's example — each with assigned managers, linked vendors, PDF download and template support, on leads as well as events ([help: worksheets](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-worksheets-work); [help: managing worksheets](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding/managing-worksheets)). **Event-level custom fields were not found**; the only documented custom fields belong to the BEO ([help: BEO overview](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-does-the-banquet-event-order-beo-tool-work)).

### BEOs are generated from panels and layouts, then signed and batch-printed

The BEO is a **generated document included only in Venue Management packages** ([help: BEO overview](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-does-the-banquet-event-order-beo-tool-work)). Under Settings → Banquet Event Order, admins define **panels** whose fields either fill themselves from event tools — F&B packages, headcounts, venues, floor plans — or are text fields with admin-set default text, plus custom fields such as "special requirements" and "load-in details" ([help: BEO default fields](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-default-fields-and-text-in-beos)). Hiding a field removes it from every BEO that uses that panel without deleting the data ([help: BEO content panels](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating/editing-content-panels-for-beo-layouts)). Panels combine into **layouts**, such as a front-of-house and a kitchen version, and **changing a layout rewrites existing event BEOs that use it** ([help: BEO settings](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-the-beo-tool-settings-in-your-account)). Inside an event, the BEO filters by location, meal type and time period before PDF download ([help: BEO filters](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-beo-filters-views-in-an-event)); a Signatures panel requests e-signatures from assigned or newly added contacts ([help: editing a BEO](https://planningpod.com/help-center/editing-a-beo-inside-an-event)); the PDF can be emailed through Communications ([help: sharing BEOs](https://planningpod.com/help-center/downloading-and-sharing-beos)); and Reports → BEO Reports → Batch Reporting zips every BEO for a chosen date ([help: batch printing BEOs](https://planningpod.com/help-center/batch-printing-beos-in-planning-pod)). Marketing says signed BEOs go through the portal with a **version history of what was signed** ([BEO software](https://planningpod.com/banquet-event-orders)).

Three limits matter for a venue. The BEO pulls proposal and invoice summaries, not the full documents ([help: BEO overview](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-does-the-banquet-event-order-beo-tool-work)); a competitor review says it handles F&B packages but not general line items such as rentals ([Perfect Venue: BEO software reviews](https://www.perfectvenue.com/post/beo-software-reviews)); and no revision numbers or "changed since signing" highlights were found. Because a layout edit rewrites live BEOs, nothing freezes a BEO at signature except that signed-version history.

### The itinerary prints a separate run-of-show for each vendor, person and room

The Itinerary tool holds **unlimited items across multiple days**, each with assigned vendors, contacts and a venue room, a colour-coded category, attachments and reminders ([help: itineraries](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-itineraries-work)). Views run chronologically or by vendor, contact, category or venue, and **filtered PDFs produce one schedule per vendor, staff member, category or room**, so the photographer receives only the photographer's items ([help: vendor itineraries](https://planningpod.com/help-center/assigning-vendors-to-itinerary-items-and-viewing/downloading-vendor-itineraries); [help: venue itineraries](https://planningpod.com/help-center/assigning-venues-to-itinerary-items-and-viewing-downloading-venue-itineraries)). A bulk "Assign Vendors/Contacts" action adds, but never removes, assignees across ticked items. Templates place each item a set number of days before, on or after the event date, and inserting an item can **shift the start times of earlier or later items by a set amount** ([help: itinerary templates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating/using-itinerary-templates); [help: itinerary items](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding/managing-an-itinerary-item)). Assigned contacts get email or text reminders, and marketing adds CSV export, spreadsheet import and prebuilt wedding templates ([schedules & itineraries](https://planningpod.com/schedules-and-itineraries)). Couples see the full itinerary in the portal ([help: for clients](https://planningpod.com/help-center/for-clients-what-is-the-client-portal-and-how-do-i-use-it)); whether items carry end times, durations or a client-visibility flag was not retrieved.

### Floor plans handle outdoor sites and let couples seat guests within venue limits

Floorplans is the most developed operations tool. Staff draw **to-scale room outlines or upload a background image** such as a site plan ([help: room outlines](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating-managing-room-outlines-background-images)), then place elements from a library of round, rectangular, square, serpentine, seminar and cocktail tables, straight or curved chair rows, stages, dance floors, podiums, lighting, A/V, doors, stairs, barriers, people, rulers and notes ([help: floorplans overview](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-floorplans-work); [seating charts](https://planningpod.com/seating-charts)), plus **tents and canopies with editable dimensions, heating and cooling units, and infrastructure and electrical elements** ([help: outdoor elements](https://planningpod.com/help-center/using-outdoor-elements-tents-canopies-heating/cooling-elements); [help: electrical elements](https://planningpod.com/help-center/using-infrastructure-and-electrical-elements)). Layout helpers include multi-add with even spacing, group align, distribute and shift, a **clear area** around each table or chair row, and auto-setup that grids tables inside the clear space ([help: clear areas](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-clear-areas-between-tables-and-chair-rows)). Each space can carry a default floor-plan template ([help: floorplan templates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating/using-floorplan-templates)).

Seating runs through an Attendees tab tied to the guest list. Staff seat guests at tables and update RSVP status, meal choice, priority and VIP flags from the plan, and **one guest list can feed several plans**, such as a ceremony and a reception layout ([help: seat assignments](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-attendee-table-and-seat-assignments); [help: attendee status in floorplans](https://planningpod.com/help-center/updating-attendee-status-meal-choices-priority-and-vip-status-in-the-floorplans-tool); [seating charts](https://planningpod.com/seating-charts)). Exports include a full-colour PDF with setup instructions, an **equipment list** that serves as the per-event table and chair count, table breakout pages, name badges and table assignments ([help: floorplans & check-in](https://planningpod.com/help-center/floorplans-event-check-in)). In the portal, couples get **view-only or edit access**; with edit access they can "make changes, seat attendees, add elements and more", but **only elements the venue has starred as favourites** are available to them ([help: portal permissions](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-permissions-and-granting-client-access-to-the-client-portal); [help: floorplans overview](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-floorplans-work)), and the venue is notified of floor-plan edits ([blog: catering package technology](https://planningpod.com/blog/planning-pod-catering-package-technology-gives-users-new-levels-of-customization)). Opinion splits: reviewers variously call floor planning the place the product "genuinely excels" and tools that look outdated beside dedicated floor-plan software, and a Capterra venue owner says "The floor plan tool isn't the easiest to work with" ([EverBridal](https://www.everbridal.com/blogs/planning-pod-for-wedding-venues); [Perfect Venue review](https://www.perfectvenue.com/post/planning-pod-review); [Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/125947/Planning-Pod/reviews/)). No 3D view, live vendor share link or seats-versus-headcount check was found.

### Guest lists split into RSVP sub-events with meals and plus-ones

Each event's Attendee List runs in one of two modes, **RSVP** or **Ticketing/Registration** ([help: RSVP vs ticketing](https://planningpod.com/help-center/attendee-management-rsvp-vs-ticketing-registration)). Staff add or CSV-upload attendees with contact details, RSVP status, meal choice and custom-question answers, and can export CSV or print name badges ([help: attendee list](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-the-attendee-list-for-collecting-rsvps-works)). One event can hold several **"RSVP Events"** — a welcome party, ceremony, reception and brunch, say — each with its own meal options and text or multiple-choice questions answered on the **Event Website** ([help: RSVP events](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding/managing-rsvp-events)). **Plus-ones** are unnamed placeholders whose names and meals the host guest supplies when replying ([help: plus ones](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-plus-ones-to-attendees)). The Check-In tool offers supervised self check-in, walk-up "Add Now" for RSVP events, and QR scanning for ticketed events only ([help: self check-in](https://planningpod.com/help-center/using-the-self-check-in-feature-in-the-check-in-tool); [help: walk-ups](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-attendees-at-the-door-via-the-check-in-tool-rsvp-events-only); [help: QR check-in](https://planningpod.com/help-center/using-a-qr-code-reader-app-to-check-in-attendees-ticketing/registration-events-only)). With portal permission, couples add guests, manage meal selections and RSVPs, and group attendees ([help: for clients](https://planningpod.com/help-center/for-clients-what-is-the-client-portal-and-how-do-i-use-it)). **No household model, dedicated dietary or allergy field, or RSVP deadline and guest reminder emails** were documented; dietary needs go into custom questions. For a venue, as opposed to a planner, the payoff is final counts, meal counts by choice and seating, which feed the BEO and the floor plan.

### To-dos anchor to event, inquiry or booking dates; checklists stay undated

Planning Pod splits tasks deliberately. **Checklists** are undated, unassigned lists; "if you need to be able to assign due dates and assign Contacts to items, use the To-Do's tool" ([help: checklist templates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating/using-checklist-templates)). **To-dos** carry multiple assignees, due dates, reminders, notes, attachments and sub-lists, with filters for incomplete, due today plus overdue and upcoming, priority, completed, list and contact ([help: to-dos](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-to-dos-work)). A template item's "Due Date & Reminder" tab places the task **before, on or after the event date, the lead-capture date or the booking date** by a number of days, or leaves it undated, and can preset an email and/or text reminder a set number of days ahead at a chosen time of day; importing the template computes real dates ([help: to-do templates](https://planningpod.com/help-center/creating-using-to-do-list-templates)). To-dos show on the calendar, flow into external calendar feeds, surface as red overdue items and export to PDF ([help: to-do PDF](https://planningpod.com/help-center/downloading-a-pdf-file-of-to-dos); [checklists & task management](https://planningpod.com/checklists-and-task-management)), and couples tick off to-dos assigned to them in the portal ([help: client portal overview](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-what-is-the-client-portal-and-how-does-it-work)). **Recurring tasks and a kanban board were not found.**

### Vendors are tagged contacts, staffing is assignment-only, inventory is marketing

There is **no vendor directory**; vendors are Contacts tagged by type. An event's Vendors tab lets vendors be assigned to budget and itinerary items and appear in worksheets and the "Assigned Vendors" PDF, but it "does not give vendors access to the event or to the account", and leads have a matching vendor list ([help: event dashboards](https://planningpod.com/help-center/using-event-dashboards); [help: lead vendor list](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-vendors-to-a-lead-vendor-list)). Access comes only from a contact's role — No Access, Super Admin, Admin, Employee, User or Client (Portal Only) — and none is a vendor role ([help: adding a user](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-a-new-user-to-your-planning-pod-account)). The legacy reports include a Vendor Summary ([help: legacy reporting](https://planningpod.com/help-center/legacy-reporting)). **No preferred-vendor list for couples, insurance-certificate tracking, vendor ratings or vendor portal** was found.

Staffing follows the same pattern: employees are contacts assigned to events and then to to-dos and itinerary items, each receiving a personal itinerary PDF and reminders ([help: productivity tools](https://planningpod.com/help-center/productivity-tools)). Shift scheduling — tools that "set days and times for which events your staff members are assigned to" — appears only on the hotel marketing page, with no help article on shifts, hours or staff conflicts ([hotels & lodging](https://planningpod.com/solutions/industries/hotels-and-lodging)). Inventory is the same story: marketing promises to "upload your inventory/rentals and allocate inventory to events" and an "Extras" allocation feature ([wedding planning firms](https://planningpod.com/solutions/industries/wedding-planning-firms); [event planning firms](https://planningpod.com/solutions/industries/event-planning-firms)), but a targeted help-center search returned no inventory article and a reviewer wishes for inventory management ([Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/125947/Planning-Pod/reviews/)). The floor-plan equipment list is the only documented per-event count, and **no per-date availability or shortfall check is documented**.

## Portal, permissions, reporting and integrations stay coarse-grained

### The client portal signs, pays, collects answers and locks edits before the date

Planning Pod calls the portal a "paired-down version" of the full account ([help: client portal overview](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-what-is-the-client-portal-and-how-does-it-work)). Couples log in to **view and e-sign proposals, contracts and BEOs; view and pay invoices** with payment schedules and receipts by card, debit or ACH; **complete forms and questionnaires**, which arrive by email when ready; view F&B menus and the itinerary; tick assigned to-dos; manage the guest list; view or edit the floor plan; exchange files; manage saved payment methods; and comment on and download from a Vision Board of images the venue uploads ([help: client portal overview](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-what-is-the-client-portal-and-how-does-it-work); [help: for clients](https://planningpod.com/help-center/for-clients-what-is-the-client-portal-and-how-do-i-use-it); [invoicing & payments](https://planningpod.com/invoicing-and-payments)). Staff invite a client by setting the contact's "User Access" to "Client (Portal Only)", which emails a link to create a login ([help: portal permissions](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-permissions-and-granting-client-access-to-the-client-portal)).

Control works at three levels: **account-wide** tool switches under Settings → User Access & Permissions; **per item**, since clients see only events they are assigned to and only the tools they are assigned to or signing in; and **by date**, through Settings → "Client Portal" → "Lock Out Changes?", which freezes client edits a set number of days before the event ([help: portal permissions](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-permissions-and-granting-client-access-to-the-client-portal); [help: client portal overview](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-what-is-the-client-portal-and-how-does-it-work)). One UK wedding venue tells its couples the lock falls 29 days out ([The Pendennis](https://www.thependennisweddingsandevents.com/planningpod)). Messaging stays in email: no in-portal chat was found, and a comparison lists Planning Pod's communication tools as "Central email inbox, reusable email templates, client portals" ([blog: HoneyBook vs Dubsado vs Planning Pod vs 17hats](https://planningpod.com/blog/honeybook-vs-dubsado-vs-planning-pod-vs-17hats-event-business-management-software-competitors-pricing)). Branding is promised as a "branded client portal" without documented custom domains ([client portals](https://planningpod.com/client-portals)). Adoption is the real risk — one reviewer's clients "will not use the features available to them because it is too confusing/complicated" ([Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/125947/Planning-Pod/reviews/)) — and how two partners, or paying parents, share one event is undocumented beyond the one-viewer invoice complaint.

### Four user groups plus event assignment govern staff access

Accounts allow **unlimited authorised users**, each in one of **four groups — Super Admin, Admin, Employee and User** — whose permissions are set per group in Settings → User Access & Permissions; all but Super Admin can be renamed, and any group can be made **read-only** ([help: system access](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-how-do-system-access-and-user-permission-settings-work); [help: permission levels](https://planningpod.com/help-center/setting-the-permission-levels-for-authorized-user-groups)). Staff are invited from their contact record with an "Invite to Collaborate" email ([help: granting access](https://planningpod.com/help-center/granting-authorized-users-with-account-access)). Event assignment decides which events a user can open, and the group decides what they can do there ([help: event access](https://planningpod.com/help-center/assigning-contacts-to-events-/-managing-event-access)). The Home Page Dashboard sorts notifications into **Key Activities, Overdue, Waiting and Upcoming** and can carry each user's own reports ([help: home page dashboard](https://planningpod.com/help-center/home-page-dashboard)). **Per-user permission overrides, an account-wide audit log, two-factor authentication and single sign-on were not found**, and a G2 reviewer reports "randomly getting logged out while we are working on proposals" ([G2 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/planning-pod/reviews)).

### Reporting pairs legacy CSV exports with a drag-and-drop report engine

The legacy layer is a set of fixed CSV downloads: Budgets Overview, Vendor Summary, Proposal and Invoice Reporting, Invoices/Deposits Overview, Invoice Line Items, **Open Invoices Aging**, Invoice Payments, Invoice Payment Reminders, **Tax Details**, Additional Fees Details and Proposals Overview ([help: legacy reporting](https://planningpod.com/help-center/legacy-reporting)), plus a Leads Detailed Overview with potential, actual and lost revenue and the Leads Won and Leads Lost summaries ([help: leads reporting](https://planningpod.com/help-center/leads-reporting)). The newer **Reporting Engine** keeps those behind a "Show Legacy Reporting" option and adds templates and **25+ data points**: choose a starting entity such as Contacts/Vendors or Leads/Events, set a date range and filters, drag columns from "Additional Data" into "Selected Data", and switch **dynamic filters** between "assigned to me" and the whole organisation ([help: reporting engine overview](https://planningpod.com/help-center/overview-planning-pod-reporting-engine); [help: custom report](https://planningpod.com/help-center/how-do-i-create-a-custom-report); [help: reporting engine](https://planningpod.com/help-center/how-to-create-customized-reporting-in-our-brand-new-reporting-engine)). Reports combine into dashboards, share by URL protected by domain or password, and export as screen or print PDF and Excel ([help: reporting engine](https://planningpod.com/help-center/how-to-create-customized-reporting-in-our-brand-new-reporting-engine); [automations & insights](https://planningpod.com/solutions/use-cases/automations-and-insights)). Planning Pod concedes that "tax rates and revenue will need separate reports" ([help: legacy reporting](https://planningpod.com/help-center/legacy-reporting)), **scheduled emailed reports were not found**, and the platform page names reporting on bookings, revenue, deposit health, sales pipeline and team performance without defining those measures ([platform](https://planningpod.com/platform)).

### QuickBooks Online syncs natively; nearly everything else needs Zapier

The only native accounting link is **QuickBooks Online**: staff choose which invoices to push, later edits sync, payments recorded in Planning Pod apply to the matching QuickBooks invoice, and tax is tracked per line ([help: QuickBooks Online](https://planningpod.com/help-center/product-update-planning-pod-integration-with-quickbooks-online)). Zapier triggers fire on new leads, invoices, to-dos, ticket payments, invoice payments and deposits, which is how venues reach Xero, Mailchimp, Constant Contact, Calendly or Google Calendar ([Zapier: Planning Pod](https://zapier.com/apps/planning-pod/integrations); [help: Zapier](https://planningpod.com/help-center/integrating-with-other-applications-via-zapier)). Calendar integration is the one-way feed and the marketplace connection is email parsing, both described above. **No public API, webhooks, QuickBooks Desktop support or native Xero connection** was found.

### Branding stops at logo and colour, and mobile means a responsive website

Branding is an organisation logo (PNG or JPG only) and home-page branding, plus per-event name, logo, colour and point of contact for registration pages ([help: logo and branding](https://planningpod.com/help-center/adding-your-organizations-logo-and-home-page-branding); [help: event branding](https://planningpod.com/help-center/managing-the-event-branding-name/date-logo-color-point-of-contact)); **white-labelling, custom portal domains, multiple languages and multiple currencies were not documented**. There is **no native mobile app**: Planning Pod describes itself as "a fully responsive application" and recommends a home-screen bookmark ([help: tablet or smartphone](https://planningpod.com/help-center/how-to-access-planning-pod-on-a-tablet-or-smartphone-device)), offline use is undocumented, and an aggregator lists a weaker mobile experience among its shortcomings ([Subscribed.fyi](https://subscribed.fyi/planning-pod/reviews/)) — a practical concern at rural venues with patchy signal on the day.

## Conclusion

Planning Pod's real advantage is propagation rather than any single feature. One headcount edit in a BEO reprices proposals, invoices and budget lines; a signed proposal becomes an invoice in a click; a lead's files, notes and documents ride along on "Book Event"; a floor plan's equipment list becomes the setup count; and one guest list feeds RSVPs, meal counts and several seating plans. A gap analysis that ticks capabilities row by row will flatter any product that has the features in isolation, so the linked pairs — guest count to pricing, proposal to invoice, guest list to seating to event order — deserve end-to-end tests: change the number once and see what moves. The evidence caveat cuts the other way for absences. They come from help-center extracts, so the few that would drive a build decision should be confirmed in a demo before they are counted as Planning Pod gaps.

The second lesson is where its weakness concentrates. The internal operations tools are Planning Pod's best-documented and least-criticised layer, while everything a couple or an email touches — the e-signature document, the inbox, bulk outreach, multiple payers, the portal's usability — is the thinnest and most complained-about. A custom venue CRM gets the most leverage there: structured contracts with initials, fill-in fields and an enforced signing order, tracked email from the venue's own domain, a permanent activity timeline, typed custom fields, payment schedules computed from the event date, seasonal package prices, multi-payer pay links and Canadian payment rails such as Interac e-Transfer all exceed what Planning Pod documents. What is worth borrowing is a short list of cheap mechanics rather than its breadth: the hold with an expiry date and an 8 am email, lock-on-signature with re-signed change orders, category-scoped service charges and minimum-headcount billing, to-dos anchored to the booking date, per-audience run-of-show PDFs with a "shift later items" control, couple seating limited to venue-approved elements, and a portal edit lock a set number of days before the wedding.
